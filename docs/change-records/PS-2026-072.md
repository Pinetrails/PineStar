## Repetition assessment

Three repetitions per task materially improve evidence quality by exposing within-task stochastic formatting, correctness, and latency variation and by allowing a two-of-three reliability pattern instead of letting one sample decide an arm/task result. A six-run design has one observation per arm/task and no repeatability evidence. A 12-run design has two observations per arm/task, detects instability, but a split result cannot distinguish an isolated failure from the dominant behavior. Both smaller designs reduce runtime, but 6 materially and 12 moderately reduce confidence.

Eighteen runs remain recommended and proportionate for a configuration-quality decision: the work is local and `$0`, the contract defect is corrected, and the decision could affect a production role, while activation still remains separately protected. A 12-run alternative is reasonable only if Commander prioritizes time over majority-pattern evidence; it would use two repetitions per task per arm, 12 slots, a 48-minute strict measured ceiling, an estimated 24–44 minutes, and a proposed 65-minute wall cap.
